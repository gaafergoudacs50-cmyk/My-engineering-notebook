00 — Why are we meeting?

I am leading a graduation project to build an autonomous telescope. I do not want to leave this meeting with only general advice. I want a practical engineering direction that my team can start implementing.

My target before leaving: a reviewed component list, preliminary electrical architecture, sensor plan, motor-control strategy, first build task, and measurable test criteria.

What does “prototype works” mean?

  • Both axes move independently and together.
  • The mount can find a repeatable home position.
  • Movement is smooth and does not skip steps under the tested load.
  • We can command a known angle and verify the result.
  • Power, wiring, and emergency stop are safe.

02 — Questions about mechanics & motors

Load and torque

  1. What is the estimated total payload on the elevation axis?
  2. How should we calculate the required torque, including friction and acceleration?
  3. What safety margin should we use?
  4. Is NEMA 17 enough for both axes, or should the elevation axis use a stronger motor?
  5. Should we balance the optical tube around the elevation axis with a counterweight?
  6. Do we need a gearbox, timing belt, worm gear, or direct drive?
  7. How do we estimate backlash and its effect on pointing accuracy?

Motion performance

  1. What maximum slew speed is reasonable for the prototype?
  2. What acceleration and deceleration profile should we use?
  3. Will microstepping improve smoothness enough for our use?
  4. How do we test whether the motor is missing steps?
  5. How do we prevent vibration from blurring the camera image?
  6. What happens if the mount reaches a mechanical limit?
  7. How should we route wires so they do not twist during 360° azimuth movement?
Ask for a calculation, not a guess: motor torque at the expected speed, estimated axis torque, gear ratio, and the assumptions used.

04 — Sensor selection & readings

MPU6050 — accelerometer + gyroscope

  • Accelerometer measures acceleration on X/Y/Z, including gravity when stationary.
  • Gyroscope measures angular rate around X/Y/Z.
  • It does not provide a reliable absolute azimuth heading by itself.

Ask: Do we really need tilt and vibration measurements in the first prototype? What filter, sample rate, mounting orientation and calibration should we use?

QMC5883L — magnetometer

  • Measures magnetic-field components on three axes.
  • Heading needs calibration and tilt compensation where appropriate.
  • Motors, steel parts, current-carrying wires and nearby magnets can distort readings.

Ask: Is a compass useful on this mount? Can we place it far from motors? How will we test hard-iron and soft-iron errors?

Limit switches

  • Provide a digital trigger at a known mechanical reference.
  • Can establish a repeatable home point if mounted and approached consistently.
  • Need debounce and safe failure handling.

Ask: Normally open or normally closed? One switch per axis or more? What happens if a switch wire breaks?

Rotary encoders

  • Measure shaft or axis rotation depending on where they are installed.
  • Can help detect missed steps and measure actual position.
  • A motor-shaft encoder may not detect gearbox backlash downstream.

Ask: Do we need axis-mounted encoders now, or can we begin with homing plus step counting and add feedback later?

Coordinate questions

  1. Which software/library calculates RA/Dec to Alt/Az?
  2. Which time standard and location accuracy are required?
  3. How do we handle local sidereal time and atmospheric refraction?
  4. How do we handle azimuth wraparound near 0°/360°?
  5. What happens when a target is below the horizon or outside elevation limits?
  6. How often should target coordinates be updated during tracking?

Motion questions

  1. How do we convert a requested angle into motor steps?
  2. How do we choose a shortest safe path?
  3. Should both axes move simultaneously?
  4. What acceleration profile is suitable?
  5. How do we detect a stalled motor?
  6. When is PID useful, and what is the measured feedback variable?

Basic step calculation — write the real values after the meeting

Full steps per motor revolution = 360° ÷ motor full-step angle.

With microstep factor μ and gear ratio G (motor turns per axis turn):

axis steps per degree = (360 ÷ step angle) × μ × G ÷ 360

Remember: this is a commanded-position estimate. It does not prove the physical axis reached that angle.

07 — Homing, calibration & safety

Homing questions

  • Where is the mechanical home position for each axis?
  • At what low speed should the axis approach the switch?
  • Do we back off and re-approach slowly for repeatability?
  • How do we debounce the switch?
  • How do we detect a switch that is permanently active?
  • How do we prevent the mount moving into a hard stop?

Calibration questions

  • How do we align the mount level?
  • How do we establish true north or a known azimuth reference?
  • How do we calibrate the elevation zero?
  • How do we measure pointing repeatability?
  • How do we account for backlash and structural flex?
  • How often is recalibration needed?
Safety decision: agree on a physical power cut-off, software limits, safe homing direction, cable management, and what the system does after a reset or sensor fault.
